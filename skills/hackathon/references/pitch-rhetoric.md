# Pitch rhetoric: what was measured, what is illustration

A checklist for the spoken script of a 2–3 minute pitch video, and for any slide line that has to land on first read. Keep the two layers apart: only the first section was tested against matched controls ([what-separates-winners.md](what-separates-winners.md)).

## Measured

Language metrics computed by script on the 43 winners' and 47 controls' pitch transcripts, Mann-Whitney, 14 metrics, no correction:

- **Density of concrete numbers separated, weakly:** a median 5.1 numbers a minute for winners vs 3.6 for controls (p 0.018). It doesn't survive correction, so treat it as a lean, not a rule.
- **Surface plainness did not separate:** sentence length (17 vs 14 words, p 0.40), Flesch reading ease (59 vs 65, p 0.41), share of short sentences, use of "you", analogies (a median of 0 in both groups).
- Not differences: total words (490 vs 413, p 0.075); words per minute (170 vs 153) was not tested.

Reading: "simple" in the winners' pitches isn't vocabulary. Each sentence carries one checkable thing (a number, a name, a date), anchored to something the listener already knows.

## Illustrative: what winners did

Recurring in winners' pitch videos. Not counted against controls, so none of these is evidence on its own.

1. **One-liner: a category noun the listener knows, plus who it's for.** "stablecoin payments for supply chain" ([CargoBill](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/cargobill)); "the human infrastructure layer for physical AI" ([CrowdBrain](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/crowdbrain)).
2. **"An alternative to X" when an incumbent exists.** "an open source alternative to Stripe … using an identical API to Stripe" ([Zoneless](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/zoneless)). The incumbent does half the explaining.
3. **Without-you vs with-you, both as numbers.** "Without TapeDrive, 10 megabytes today would set you back around 72 Sol." Then: "1400 times less." ([TapeDrive](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/tapedrive)); "a 3% merchant fee, which is on average 2 to 3x less than Klarna" ([Yumi Finance](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/yumi-finance)).
4. **The problem from the founder's own chair, with a number.** "I was paying $9,400 per month using Stripe Connect to do payouts" ([Zoneless](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/zoneless)).
5. **Every number with its unit, its time window and whose it is.** "grown 100% month-over-month for 4 consecutive months" ([DashX](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/dashx-1)); "We launched 1.5 months ago and we're doing 8k in MRR." ([stablecorp](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/stablecorp)).
6. **The mechanism in one causal sentence.** "rather than having … one miner win every 10 minutes, … everyone can win as long as they can land a valid hash on chain" ([Ore](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/ore)). Shape: today it works by A; A breaks because of B; we do C; so D.
7. **Why now, or "it can't happen until…".** "prediction markets can't become a real asset class until users are actually able to build portfolios, hedge risks properly" ([Senthos](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/senthos)). A counterintuitive customer fact works too: "merchants prefer QR codes" ([LocalPay](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/localpay)).
8. **Proof narrated in the present tense, as it happens.** "we can just check the transaction which was successful" ([Reflect](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/reflect-protocol)). Present tense only for what has happened; see the tense rule in [pitch-deck](../../pitch-deck/SKILL.md#3-narrative-spine).
9. **Name the obvious objection, then the defence.** "Flip side of fractional reserves is a possible bank run scenario." Then: "We address this with a FIFO Q mechanism" ([Kormos](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/kormos)).
10. **One concrete use case.** "a staking provider can target specific users who are not staking with a unique offer" ([Torque](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/torque)).
11. **A credential is one specific fact, with a number, tied to the problem.** "Our team from Baidu Global has years of experience with top keyboard apps like FaceMoji and Symoji which reached 330 million daily active users", for a keyboard product ([TypeX](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/typex-keyboard)). This one is measured: see the credential row in [what-separates-winners.md](what-separates-winners.md#what-separated-robustly).
12. **Spoken signposts, and the one-liner again at the close.** "So what is TapeDrive?" ([TapeDrive](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/tapedrive)); "This has once again been the pitch for Zoneless, which is a Stripe Connect alternative." ([Zoneless](https://colosseum.com/projects/explore/zoneless)).

## Illustrative: how pitches lose

Patterns seen in control pitches. The shapes below are invented to show the pattern, not quotes.

- A claim with no who, no number and no consequence ("[The market] needs a decentralised [thing]").
- Stacked buzzwords ("a zk-powered DePIN layer for onchain data intelligence").
- A superlative or projection with nothing under it ("we'll capture 10% of the market next year").
- Self-assessment in place of evidence ("a really engaging experience").
- A sentence shaped like traction with nothing behind it ("several ongoing enterprise discussions").
- Belief offered as a market argument ("this will be bigger than the internet").
- A generic "why Solana" ("it's fast and cheap"), or one dropped at the very end.
- A feature list or UI tour with no problem and no user.
- Handing work to the viewer ("pause the video and read the roadmap").
- Shrinking your own problem ("there's a small issue with…").

## Checklist for the script

- [ ] **Measured:** traction, said with a number. Give the number its unit, its window and its owner. Without traction, the checkable artifact or the record ([the other way in](what-separates-winners.md#no-traction-yet-the-other-way-in)).
- [ ] **Measured:** a team credential. Make it one fact per founder, tied to the problem; "I study X" is a credential with no fact.
- [ ] **Measured, weak:** about five concrete numbers a minute, each one sourced.
- [ ] One-liner in the first sentences, repeated at the close. "Alternative to X" if an incumbent exists.
- [ ] Without-you vs with-you, both as numbers.
- [ ] The problem from your own chair, with a number.
- [ ] The mechanism in one causal sentence.
- [ ] Why now, in one sentence.
- [ ] The obvious objection named, with the defence.
- [ ] Present tense only for what is measured; the roadmap in the future tense.
- [ ] None of the losing patterns above.
